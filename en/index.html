SAVERS · Evacuation guidance for disaster-vulnerable residents

Team Lead · PM · QA · Infra (team of 4) · K-Digital Training Hackathon 2026

  • Shelter choice and walking routes are computed by rule-based code (OSMnx / NetworkX), not the LLM; the LLM only explains results that are already fixed.
  • Pass bar: zero claims that are not in the event log. 30/30 runtime answers passed, and 12/12 adversarial prompts were blocked.
  • In a 52-person survey, evacuation intent rose from 3.2 to 8.3 (p<0.001); won the Minister of Employment and Labor Award (Encouragement Prize).
More
  • Tested both wrong refusals and missed refusals: the chatbot wrongly refused only 1 of 28 valid questions (3.6%), but answered all 6 out-of-scope questions from unrelated evidence, a failure grounding scores do not catch; traced it to the retrieval step, not the verifier.
  • Contributions: built the claim-level verifier, the golden-set evaluation and the AWS/CI deployment; first-authored the paper.
  • Stack: FastAPI, LangChain, AWS EC2

Jun – Jul 2026

BidMate · RAG Q&A and summarization for public RFPs

Evaluation (team of 5) · LangChain, LangGraph, FAISS, KURE-v1, EXAONE 3.5

  • 100 public RFPs in HWP and PDF; answers must cite the source and refuse unanswerable questions.
  • Built a 95-question golden set (5 types, 19 RFPs) with type-specific scorers; compared 708 configurations in 3 stages (retrieval grid, answer eval, controlled re-test).
  • Final local stack (KURE-v1 + dense k=4 + EXAONE 3.5): MRR 0.977, Hit@k 1.0, refusal 1.0 (19/19), 3.6 s, no API cost.
More
  • OpenAI embeddings ranked last in the earlier 50-question round (MRR 0.65) and were dropped.
  • Found that a score stuck at 0.3 came from errors in the golden set, not the model; raised Ollama's 4,096-token context limit to 8,192 after finding it cut off evidence; removed a reranker that added 21 s of latency for no gain.
  • Contributions: ran the embedding comparison, designed the golden-set and RAGAS evaluation, and wrote the automated scoring scripts.

Apr – May 2026

PillScope · Object detection for oral medication images

Team Lead · PM (team of 5) · YOLOv11, RT-DETRv2, RF-DETR, COCO

  • 56 look-alike pill classes, only 186 training images, a 51× class imbalance, and up to 4 pills per image.
  • Minority-class crop-and-paste grew the data from 186 to 1,026 images (mAP@0.5 0.886 → 0.964).
  • YOLOv11l reached validation mAP@[0.75:0.95] 0.936, ahead of RF-DETR v3 (0.882) and RT-DETRv2 (0.876); a 3-model WBF ensemble raised the in-course Kaggle score from 0.851 to 0.9355 (+8.5 pp).
More
  • Grouped near-duplicate shots into the same split to prevent train/validation leakage, then ran 7 staged experiments across 4 detectors.
  • Compositing web pill images onto the competition background raised mAP@[0.75:0.95] from 0.919 to 0.936 and made a never-detected rare class detectable.
  • Contributions: PM; built the COCO → YOLO pipeline, YOLOv11l training, DETR experiments and crop-and-paste augmentation.

Fall 2019

Biomedical Abstract Sentence Classification · BERT vs. SciBERT Embeddings

Course project, Penn State IST 597 Foundations of Deep Learning · team of 2, equal contribution · advised by Dr. C. Lee Giles · TensorFlow

  • Problem: most biomedical abstracts are unstructured, so readers cannot jump straight to the methods or results they need.
  • Extracted BERT/SciBERT token embeddings from model checkpoints to replace Word2Vec (5.4M entries → 31K) in a hierarchical BiLSTM-CRF sentence classifier (HSLN), and ran all five conditions, including the baseline re-run.
  • On PubMed 20k RCT (5 classes), matched the re-run baseline (test accuracy 92.43% vs. 92.35%, single run) with a ~175× smaller embedding table; identified low vocabulary overlap (16% vs. 68% for Word2Vec) as the bottleneck.
